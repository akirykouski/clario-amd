## Business value

**Buyer.** The clinic or hospital department. **User on the other side.** The patient, free.

**What the clinic gets.** A structured pre-visit dossier (terms the patient did not reject, with the patient's own quotes, a timeline, imported lab history) and a reviewable brief where every AI line carries a citation and a clinician decision.

**Value hypothesis, to be measured.** The brief reduces the time a clinician spends rebuilding the history at the start of a visit. We do not claim a number. The pilot measures minutes to first useful question with and without the dossier, and clinician acceptance rate per brief item (the clinic console counts lines accepted, edited and rejected per brief and the time from opening a package to finalize, at `GET /api/clinic/metrics`, shown on the inbox header and the brief panel).

**Pricing hypothesis, untested.** Per clinic subscription for the clinic console and the on-premise model server, set after pilot interviews: `{{PRICE_HYPOTHESIS}}`. The patient app stays free so that dossiers keep arriving. A hospital that prefers not to run a GPU can use a managed AMD-hosted clinic instance under a data processing agreement.

**Go-to-market in Italy.**

1. Start with reference centres for rare and undiagnosed diseases, where the diagnostic wait is longest and the visit is already a structured, multi-specialty process.
2. Work through patient associations (for example UNIAMO, the Italian federation of rare diseases) to recruit pilot patients, using the synthetic demo first.
3. Run a 90-day pilot at one or two centres with a measurement plan agreed upfront.
4. Italian and English from day one (the app and the data generator already handle both).
5. Expand by reference network once acceptance and time data exist.

## Judge Q&A prep (12 questions)

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is this a medical device? | Our position: no. Clario is a patient organiser and a note summariser. It does not give a diagnosis, a risk score or treatment advice, and code enforces that (hedge validator, Clario never shows a patient a condition name it inferred, and no percentages). A regulatory assessment with a qualified advisor comes before any clinical deployment. Detail in `docs/COMPLIANCE.md`. This is our position and it is not legal advice |
| 2 | Why not just use GPT or Claude? | Three reasons. Health text is Article 9 data, so the clinic model runs on the clinic's own server and the patient side replaces direct identifiers by rules before any remote call (misses 10.8% of identifiers on our strictly held-out synthetic test half, 5.8% after fixes chosen by reading test failures, `eval/results/pii_leak.json`, n = 712). Second, the extractor is a small model (E4B) with a laptop export path (`train/export.py`, not yet measured on a laptop). Third, the guardrails are in code (quotes, ontology, citations), so the base model matters less than the checks around it. We use Claude only to draft gold labels for human review and as an eval comparison, never for training |
| 3 | Where does the data go? | The diary stays in an encrypted vault on the patient's device. A share sends only the entries from a chosen period and their terms (never the ones the patient rejected), sealed to the clinic's key, expiring. The clinic stores the opened package on its own server. The privacy page lists every model call and its hash chain. Before text reaches a remote model, Clario replaces direct identifiers with placeholders using rules and a first-name lexicon, with no model involved: emails, phone numbers, codice fiscale, tessera sanitaria and hospital record numbers, street addresses, full dates including dates of birth, and personal names recognised by title, label, relation, first-name list or "X said" pattern. Medical eponyms, doses, lab values, drug names and places of care are kept. On a synthetic English and Italian test set it misses 10.8% of identifiers when strictly held out, and 5.8% after fixes chosen by reading test failures (`eval/results/pii_leak.json`, n = 712); first names that open a sentence and are not in the lexicon are the largest gap. Scanned images and photos are sent unredacted; the preview shows the exact text the model receives. On the public demo everything is synthetic |
| 4 | How do you know the model is right? | We check it. Each term must quote the patient's words, each ontology id must match those words, and uncertain ones go to the patient. On the numbers: `{{extraction.ft-e4b-r16.entity_f1}}` entity F1 and `{{extraction.ft-e4b-r16.quote_grounding_rate}}` grounding on the held-out set, from `eval/results`. Known limit: part of the eval data is synthetic, so we also report a hand-reviewed gold set |
| 5 | Your training data is synthetic. Does it transfer? | Unproven, and we say so. Labels come from the ontology by construction, with disease-disjoint splits. We test on a hand-reviewed gold set and on PsyTAR mapped to HPO. The gap between the synthetic and the real rows in `eval/RESULTS.md` is the honest answer |
| 6 | What stops it from diagnosing? | Four layers. Condition names are clinician-only: patients see one card titled by the features they recorded, in everyday words. Pattern scores and the patient text come from code and a template, and the model never sees a condition name for the patient. A validator rejects "you have", "confirms", doses and percentages and falls back to a template. A clinician must accept every AI line before finalize |
| 7 | What did AMD actually do here? | The deployment target is one MI300X: the clinic brief on Gemma 4 31B on vLLM with ROCm, lab import vision calls in parallel, live GPU telemetry in the app. The stack is validated off-box and has not run on the card yet. Data generation, the LoRA fine-tune and throughput are measured on the AMD droplet by `bootstrap.sh --synth N --train --bench`; numbers land in `eval/results` and `bench/results`, and until they exist we say "planned" |
| 8 | Why would a clinic pay? | Our hypothesis is time and quality at the start of the visit. We have not measured it. The pilot is designed to measure minutes to first useful question and the clinician acceptance rate per item |
| 9 | What about a wrong or missing term in the brief? | Missing terms are the patient's to add, wrong ones the patient or clinician rejects. Rejected items never count and never leave the device. Every decision writes a correction record, which is also our improvement signal |
| 10 | What happens in an emergency? | The diary is not an emergency service and says so. Text that describes chest pain with warning signs, trouble breathing, stroke signs, heavy bleeding, a long seizure or self-harm gets an immediate notice with 112 before anything else. A dosing interceptor exists in `backend/clario/safety.py` (`check_entry`): a question about doses, stopping or changing a medicine, or a medicine in pregnancy gets an "ask your clinician or pharmacist" notice, is saved, and never reaches a model. AI text with dose or treatment wording is rejected by the validator |
| 11 | Is this just Phenomizer or LIRICAL? | No, they solve a different problem, and we say where the overlap is. Phenomizer ranks diseases by information-content semantic similarity between a clinician's HPO query and disease annotations, with a p-value from random queries. LIRICAL ranks diseases by likelihood ratios per observed and excluded phenotype, optionally with genotype, and reports a post-test probability. Both take a clinician's term list as input. Clario is a patient-side capture and a clinician-gated brief: the patient writes lay text, confirms each term against the quoted words, and the clinic reviews every AI line. Our ranker (`backend/clario/patterns.py`) scores information-content weighted best-match overlap against HPO disease annotations, counting close matches only, with at least three specific terms for a card, and shows weak, moderate or strong. It reports no probability. It is decision support for the clinician only, and the patient sees no condition name |
| 12 | Why not use a likelihood-ratio method for the ranking? | A likelihood ratio needs trustworthy frequency data and a prior. Most HPO annotations carry no frequency (the build writes a default of 0.5 when there is none) and our input is patient-reported and noisy, so a post-test probability would claim more than we know. We do not report one. We have not benchmarked our ranker against Phenomizer or LIRICAL on a shared case set, and no such comparison exists in `eval/results`. Doing it on public phenopacket cases is future work |
